# chirk: language and brand (as of Nov 16, 2026)

How we talk about chirk: what it is, what to call things, and how it sounds. Nothing here is final; it's the working language for the site, emails, and a future product page. How things *look* lives in DESIGN.md.

## What chirk is

**chirk is a simple way to make plans with your people.**

Longer: *chirk makes it easy to share a plan with your people, from posting up somewhere for an hour to planning the whole party. Make the plan, send one link, and everyone has the details and an easy way to join.*

Conversational: *Send a chirk when something's happening. It can be as simple as where you're posted up, or as detailed as the whole party.*

Shorthand (internal, maybe a tagline): **From posted up to planned out.**

Brand idea: **A little signal that something's happening.**

Philosophy: **Make the plan. Send the link. Go do the thing.**

## The words

| Word | Means | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **chirk** | the product | Always lowercase, even at the start of a sentence. Not "the chirk app" or "Chirk". |
| **chirk\*** | the wordmark | The name with the spark. Used as the logo, the home page and the footer. |
| **the spark** | the asterisk | A small signal: something's happening. Later it can softly blink when there's something new (like a message-waiting light), never a red badge or a count. |
| **a chirk** | one plan, with its own link | "Send a chirk", "I sent you a chirk." Let the noun catch on naturally; don't force verbs ("chirk up") into the interface. |
| **your people** | the people you make plans with | Past guests and, later, friends and circles. |
| **friends** | two-way: both people agree | Later. |
| **circles** | private, named groups ("Thanksgiving crew") | Later. Choose who sees a chirk, and whose chirks you see. |
| **posted up** | a light chirk: where you are, for a while | Later. "Hudson · Sey Coffee · now → 2". Intent, not tracking. |

### Words in the app today (keep them consistent)
- **Hosts / Guests.** Hosts make and run a chirk (owner and co-hosts); guests reply.
- **Going / Can't make it / No reply yet.** No "Maybe" for now (it produces piles of non-answers; revisit later).
- **Still needed / Covered** for the bring list; **Coming** for who's coming; **Notes** are guests' messages.
- **Message** is the host's words on the invite; **Message your guests** is a separate email, apart from the invite.
- **Hosted by** signs the message ("— Hudson & Molly").
- **By invitation only** in the footer: chirk is reached through links people send.
- **Host** label on the guest list: hosts are on their own list as going, but never counted as a reply.
- **Costs:** "Split between", "Settle up" (hosts) / "Your share" (guests), "owes", "Pay with Venmo", "Mark paid", "Everyone's even ✓" / "You're even ✓".
- **Poker night:** Buy-in / Cash-out, "Buy in all", In · Out · Net, "counted as the $X left on the table" for a worked-out cash-out.
- **Gift exchange:** Who's in, Never draw each other, Rules, Draw names, "You're giving a gift to", "Your gift ideas".
- **Sign in:** "Been invited to an event? You're invited to chirk." Anyone on a chirk guest list can sign in.
- **Templates:** "Start from a template", "Started from …", "Start blank". A template only fills in settings and example wording.
- **Scheduled sends** show their state: "sent ✓", or "skipped (too late)".

### An open question
The app says **event** today ("New event", "Your events"). Once light chirks exist (a coffee-shop "posted up"), "event" will feel too formal. The likely move: **"New chirk"** and **"Your chirks"**, everywhere at once, when the first light chirk ships. Not before.

## What chirk is not
Not, first and foremost: an RSVP tool, an event app, a location-sharing app, a social network, or a party-planning platform. Those are things chirk can *do*. The underlying behavior is simpler: **letting your people know something is happening, and giving them an easy way to join.**

## Why it exists
Plans fall into a gap. For something casual, a full invitation feels like too much; for something complicated, a group text isn't enough. So people bounce between texts, spreadsheets, invite tools, maps, calendars and notes. chirk covers the whole range with one object and one link.

## Principles that shape the words
- **A plan can start tiny.** Don't start with "What kind of event is this?" Start with "What's happening?" and add only what's needed.
- **The network grows out of real life.** Never "Find friends, build a profile, follow people." Someone hosts, people reply, and chirk remembers them.
- **Plans, not whereabouts.** chirk shares intent ("this is where the plan is"), never continuous location.
- **Before, not after.** Most social apps record what happened. chirk exists beforehand: "I'm doing this. You're welcome to join." Its job is to get people off their phones and into the same place.
- **Phone first; the link is the product.** Receive a link in a text → tap → understand the plan → reply. No download, no guest account, no password.
- **Finite and calm.** Chronological, no algorithm, no infinite scroll, no public performance. "And then the page ends."

## Voice
- Plain words, short sentences. Say what happens ("Send to 12 guests", "Sent ✓").
- Warm, not cute. No emoji in the interface. No jokes in errors, confirmations or anything about someone's reply.
- Neutral examples and defaults, never tied to one kind of event.
- Lowercase **chirk** everywhere, including emails and the start of sentences.
